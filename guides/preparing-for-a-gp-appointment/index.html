Preparing for a GP appointment

Appointments are short, and it’s easy to walk out having forgotten the question you most wanted to ask. A little preparation makes the time more useful for you and your doctor.

General informationThis guide is general organisational information, not medical advice. Do not start, stop or change a medicine based on a record or this guide. Speak with your doctor, pharmacist or another qualified health professional. In an emergency, call 000.

Most GP appointments are around fifteen minutes. Arriving with your main concern clear, your information ready and your questions written down helps you and your doctor use that time well.

Decide on your main concern

If you have several things to discuss, decide which matters most and raise it first, rather than at the end. If you have more than one or two issues, ask for a longer appointment when you book.

When you book, it’s also worth asking about fees if you’re not sure whether the appointment will be bulk billed, so there are no surprises on the day.

Write down your symptoms

Memory is unreliable under pressure. Before the appointment, note:

  • what the symptom is and when it started
  • how often it happens and how long it lasts
  • what makes it better or worse
  • anything you’ve already tried
  • how it’s affecting sleep, work or daily life

Bring your medicines list

Bring a current list of everything you take, including over-the-counter medicines, vitamins and supplements, with doses. If you keep a medication history, bring that too. Our guide to keeping an accurate medication history explains what’s useful to record.

Prepare your questions

Write your questions down in order of importance. healthdirect’s free Question Builder helps you prepare questions for an appointment and print or email them. Useful questions include:

  • What do you think is causing this?
  • What are my options, and what are the benefits and risks of each?
  • Do I need any tests, and how will I get the results?
  • What should I watch for, and when should I come back?

During the appointment

Start with your main concern. Take notes, or ask whether you can record the key points. If you don’t understand something, ask your doctor to explain it another way; that’s a normal and reasonable request. You’re welcome to bring a support person.

Telehealth appointments

For phone or video appointments, prepare in the same way, and a little more. Find a quiet, private space, check your device is charged and connected, and have your medicines list, notes and questions in front of you. If you might need to show something, such as a rash, make sure you’ll have good light.

Before you leave

Check you know what happens next: any prescriptions, referrals or tests, when to follow up, and who will contact whom about results. If you use My Health Record, some documents from your care, such as prescriptions or test results, may also appear there.

Afterwards

Write a short note of what was agreed while it’s fresh, update your medicines list if anything changed, and add follow-up appointments to your calendar.

Going with someone else

If you’re attending with a child, a parent or someone you care for, agree beforehand who will speak about what, and bring their medicines list and notes as well as yours. Our guide to organising health information for someone you care for covers what to keep on hand.

If it can’t waitIn an emergency, call 000. For health advice from a registered nurse, call healthdirect on 1800 022 222.
