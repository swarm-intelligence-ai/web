Planning a school term in one sitting

An hour before term starts saves many small scrambles later. Here’s a simple routine for getting the whole term’s dates, costs and jobs in one place.

By Swarm LabsUpdated 25 September 20263 min read

  2. Build a term overview
  3. Check the gear once
  4. Estimate the term’s costs

Most term-time stress comes from things that were known weeks in advance but surfaced the night before: a sports uniform, a consent form, a book fee. A single planning session at the start of term turns those into ordinary tasks with dates.

Before you sit down

Gather everything that describes the coming term:

  • the school’s term calendar and any newsletters already sent
  • sport, music and activity schedules for each child
  • outside school hours care bookings
  • your own work roster or travel for the term
  • any fees or payment requests you’ve been sent

Build a term overview

Make a single page or view with one row per week of term. For each week, note events (sports day, excursions, pupil-free days, parent–teacher interviews) and deadlines separately. Deadlines are the part that goes wrong: a consent form is often due well before the event it’s for.

Pupil-free days and public holidays deserve special attention. They’re easy to forget and usually need care arranged.

A few rows from a term overview might look like this:

Week 1
Sport registration closes Friday (owner: Alex). Book outside school hours care for the pupil-free day in week 4.
Week 3
Excursion consent and payment due Wednesday (owner: Sam). Library day moves to Thursday.
Week 4
Pupil-free day Monday: care booked. Swimming carnival Friday: bring towel, hat and lunch.
Week 7
Parent-teacher interviews: bookings open Monday, close Wednesday.

Check the gear once

Do a single check of uniforms, shoes, stationery, devices and sports equipment at the start of term, rather than discovering gaps one morning at a time. Make one list of what’s missing, and one trip or order to fix it.

Estimate the term’s costs

Excursions, sport fees, book club orders, fundraisers, dress-up days and camps add up. When you build the overview, note any known costs and make a rough allowance for the ones that usually appear. Setting that amount aside at the start of term, in the same way as other irregular bills, stops a run of small requests from derailing the family budget. Our guide to budgeting around irregular bills explains the approach.

Give every job an owner

For each deadline, decide now who will handle it. In a two-parent or co-parenting household, this is the step that turns a shared calendar into shared work. Write the name next to the job, not just the date.

Share it with the other adults involved

If children move between homes, or grandparents and other carers help with pick-ups, give them the parts of the overview that affect them. A short weekly message with the coming week’s changes is often more useful than access to the whole calendar.

Book the weekly check-in

Put a recurring fifteen-minute slot in the calendar, such as Sunday evening, to look at the coming fortnight. New notices go into the term overview, finished jobs come off, and anyone who owns a job that week knows about it.

Close the term properly

In the last week, spend ten minutes noting what worked and what didn’t: the deadline that caught you out, the activity that clashed, the payment you nearly missed. Those notes make next term’s planning session faster.

Good to know

This guide is general information for organising family life. Adapt it to your household and your school’s own requirements.
