Keeping an accurate medication history

A useful record is factual, consistent and easy to share when you choose. It doesn’t need to interpret your medicines or replace a health professional.

General informationThis guide is general organisational information, not medical advice. Do not start, stop or change a medicine based on a record or this guide. Speak with your doctor, pharmacist or another qualified health professional. In an emergency, call 000.

Keep a current medicines list

Record each medicine’s name, strength, form and the directions on the label. Include regular, occasional and over-the-counter medicines, and vitamins and supplements where relevant. If you’re unsure, ask your pharmacist to help you check the list is complete.

What to record each time

  • the medicine and dose, as written on the label
  • the date and time you took it
  • anything unusual, such as taking it late, with food, or a missed dose
  • a short, factual note about how you felt, if you’ve been asked to monitor something

Record what happened, not what you think it means

A history is most useful when it separates facts from interpretation. Write down the date and time, the dose you took, and a short observation in plain language. Avoid deciding that one event caused another.

Factual
“Dose taken at 8:05 am. Headache noted at 2:10 pm.”
Interpretive
“The medicine caused a headache.” Leave that conclusion to a qualified professional.

Use consistent prompts

If you’re tracking how you feel over time, use the same questions at roughly the same times each day. Consistency makes the record easier to read. Follow any instructions your clinician has given about what to monitor.

Include changes and missed doses honestly

Record changes to your medicines, missed doses and anything you’re unsure about. If you can’t remember whether you took a dose, record that uncertainty rather than guessing.

Paper or app?

Either works if you use it consistently. Paper is simple and private, but harder to summarise or share. An app can prompt you at the right times and produce a tidy summary, but check where it stores your information and whether that suits you. The best record is the one you’ll actually keep.

Review it weekly

Once a week, look back over the record. Fill in anything you missed while you still remember, and check the medicines list still matches what you’re taking. A short weekly review keeps the record accurate without it becoming a daily burden.

Prepare for an appointment

Before an appointment, check your list is current and bring it in a form you can easily show. A short summary helps, but keep the full history so questions can be checked against the detail. Our guide to preparing for a GP appointment covers the rest.

Protect the record

Medication information is sensitive. Use your device’s security features, know where backups are stored, and think about how an exported file will be protected once it leaves the app or notebook. Share it only with people and services you choose.

When to seek help

For urgent symptoms or emergencies in Australia, call 000. For concerns about a medicine, contact your doctor or pharmacist. You can also call healthdirect on 1800 022 222 for health advice from a registered nurse. A tracking tool isn’t an emergency or clinical service.
