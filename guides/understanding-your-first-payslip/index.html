Understanding your first payslip

Your payslip is the record of what you earned, what was deducted and where it went. Five minutes with it each pay cycle catches most mistakes while they’re still easy to fix.

A payslip isn’t just a receipt. It’s the evidence you’d rely on if your pay, super or leave were ever wrong. Reading it properly from your first job builds a habit that protects you for the rest of your working life.

When you should get one

In Australia, employers covered by the national workplace system must give you a payslip within one working day of paying you, including when you’re on leave. It can be on paper or electronic, but an electronic payslip must contain the same information.

What it must show

The Fair Work Ombudsman sets out what a payslip has to include. Check that yours shows:

  • your employer’s name and ABN, and your name
  • the pay period and the date you were paid
  • your gross pay (before tax) and net pay (what reached your account)
  • your hourly rate and the hours you worked, if you’re paid by the hour
  • any loadings, allowances, penalty rates or bonuses
  • each deduction, with the name of the fund or account it went to
  • superannuation contributions, and the fund they were paid to

Leave balances are often shown too. That’s good practice, but it isn’t required.

Gross pay, net pay and the gap between them

The difference between gross and net pay is mostly tax withheld by your employer and sent to the ATO on your behalf. How much is withheld depends partly on the tax file number declaration you filled in when you started. Most people claim the tax-free threshold from their main job only; if you have two jobs, check with the ATO before claiming it twice.

Other deductions only appear if you’ve agreed to them, such as salary sacrifice, union fees or a workplace giving program. If you have a study loan, your employer may also withhold extra once your income passes the repayment threshold.

Superannuation

Your employer’s super contribution is paid on top of your wages, not taken out of them. The superannuation guarantee rate is 12% of ordinary time earnings.

Since 1 July 2026, employers must pay super at the same time they pay wages, and it needs to reach your fund within seven business days. Your first contribution with a new employer can take up to 20 business days. Log in to your super fund every few months and check that the contributions on your payslips have actually arrived.

Casual, part-time and full-time differences

If you’re employed as a casual, you’ll usually see a casual loading on your payslip. Under most awards it’s 25%, paid in place of entitlements like paid annual leave and personal leave. Part-time and full-time employees don’t receive the loading, but they do build up paid leave.

Penalty rates, such as higher rates for weekend, public holiday or late-night work, should appear as separate lines, so you can check them against your award or agreement. The Fair Work Ombudsman’s pay tools can help you check the minimum rates that apply to you.

Leave balances

Under the National Employment Standards, full-time employees are entitled to four weeks of paid annual leave and ten days of paid personal or carer’s leave each year. Part-time employees receive the same entitlements on a pro-rata basis. If your payslip shows leave balances, check they’re growing each pay cycle.

A five-minute check each payday

  • Are the hours and pay rate right, including weekend or evening rates?
  • Are all the deductions ones you agreed to?
  • Does the super amount look right, and is it going to the fund you chose?
  • Does the net pay match what arrived in your bank account?

Keep your payslips. Save them to a folder or take a photo each time. They’re the easiest way to answer questions at tax time or when applying for a loan or lease.

At tax time

Most employers report your pay and tax to the ATO each payday through Single Touch Payroll. After the end of the financial year, your employer finalises that information and it appears as an income statement in your ATO online account, which you can access through myGov. Compare it with your final payslip for June. If they don’t match, ask your employer before you lodge.

If something looks wrong

Start with your manager or payroll team, in writing if possible. Most errors are honest mistakes and are fixed in the next pay. If a problem isn’t resolved, the Fair Work Ombudsman provides free information and can help. Their Infoline is 13 13 94.
