Medication records

Keeping an accurate medication history

A useful record is factual, consistent and easy to share when you choose. It does not need to interpret the medicine or replace a health professional.

7 minute readGeneral organisational informationUpdated 22 September 2026
Important: This is general organisational information, not medical advice. Do not start, stop or change medication based on a diary or this article. Speak with a doctor, pharmacist or other qualified health professional.

Keep a current medication list

Record the medication name, strength, form and the directions on the label. Include regular, occasional and over-the-counter medicines, vitamins and supplements where relevant. If you are unsure, ask a pharmacist to help you reconcile the list.

Record what happened, not what you think it means

A history is most useful when it separates facts from interpretation. Write down the date and time, the dose you recorded, and a short observation in plain language. Avoid deciding that one event caused another.

Factual: “Dose recorded at 8:05 am. Headache noted at 2:10 pm.”
Interpretive: “The medicine caused a headache.” Leave that conclusion to a qualified professional.

Use consistent prompts

If you are tracking an experience over time, use the same prompts and roughly the same check-in times. Consistency makes the record easier to understand. Follow any instructions your clinician has provided about what to monitor.

Include changes and missed doses honestly

Record changes to the medication list, missed doses and uncertainty. If you cannot remember whether a dose was taken, record that uncertainty instead of guessing.

Prepare for an appointment

Before an appointment, check that the current list is accurate and bring the information in a format you can access. A short summary can help, but retain the underlying history so questions can be checked against the detail.

Protect the record

Medication information is sensitive. Use device security, understand where backups are stored and consider how an exported file will be protected after it leaves the app or notebook. Share it only with people and services you choose.

When to seek help

For urgent symptoms or emergencies in Australia, call 000. For concerns about a medicine, contact your doctor, pharmacist or another qualified health professional. A tracking tool is not an emergency or clinical service.
